### More Hip Hop, Please! Exploring the Intersections of Public Theology and Hip Hop Studies
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20250007](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20250007)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  So far, there are only a few points of contact between public theology and Hip Hop, let alone a systematic exploration of Hip Hop’s potential contributions to public theology or insights into mutual learnings between Hip Hop Studies and public theology. This article addresses this gap by conceptually identifying common ground between Hip Hop Studies and public theology. Building on this foundation, it posits that public theology could learn from Hip Hop Studies (1) to include Hip Hop as an important witness to the lived reality of marginalized individuals in their own reflections; (2) to have more courage for positionality, (3) and to give greater consideration to aesthetic formats in their own reflections. Conversely, Hip Hop Studies can learn from public theology (1) to consider the aspect of Hip Hop’s publicity from the outset in their analysis and (2) to adopt a critical perspective towards problematic elements in Hip Hop.

### A Christian-Ethical Defence of Truth in a Post-Truth Age: in Critical Dialogue with David P. Gushee and Nikolai Berdyaev
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20250008](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20250008)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / Contemporary`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  The general mood of epistemological skepticism that characterizes the postmodern condition has created a dangerous climate of relativism in which authoritarian political leaders can commit atrocities with apparent impunity. Political leaders, especially authoritarian ‘strongmen’, have artfully exploited the postmodern propensity to regard truth not as an objective reality or a social virtue, but as a mere expression of the will to power. According to the nihilistic logic of propaganda, blatant falsehoods can be morally justified on the grounds that they contribute to the ‘greater good’ of promoting the perceived interests of the authoritarian regime. What matters in propaganda is not what is true and good, but what is profitable and beneficial to those in power. Against this postmodern nihilistic ethic, I propose (in critical dialogue with David P. Gushee and Nikolai Berdyaev) an account of truth, established within a biblical-theological convictional framework, that aims to subvert the conventional postmodern wisdom that truth is nothing more than a created value that serves the interests of power.

### Gender Relations in Korea Today in the Light of Schleiermacher’s Ethics of Solidarity and the Unfolding of the Holy Spirit
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20250010](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20250010)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / Modern`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  This study examines Schleiermacher’s perspective on Christian gender relations in relation to the context of Korean society, where conflicts between men and women are increasingly prevalent. It seeks to reconcile such conflicts. Schleiermacher considers gender relations in terms of the unfolding of humanity, which involves recognizing the individuality of both men and women and their collaboration. Christians bear the Holy Spirit in accordance with their individuality and express its unity through mutual communication. Christian gender relations are to be understood in terms of the unfolding of the Holy Spirit, whereby Christians sanctify each other through their communication, forming His unity and advancing through their union. With its critical reinterpretation, Schleiermacher’s model of Christian gender relations, understood in solidarity for the unfolding of the Holy Spirit as the meta-perspective, highlights the necessity of collaboration and solidarity between men and women for the common good in Korea.

### Christians and Nationalism in India: A Theological Engagement in Public Sphere
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20250011](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20250011)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  Nationalism in India has become a highly sensitive issue, particularly in the context of its diverse, multi-religious population. Religious nationalist ideologies have sought to define the nation, citizenship, and loyalty based on religious identity, challenging the secular nationalist ideals that once united Indians in their struggle against colonial rule. This article argues that Christians cannot support any form of nationalism that excludes fellow citizens on the grounds of religion or ethnicity, as such exclusivist rhetoric is fundamentally incompatible with Christian teachings. Nationalist ideologies that promote exclusivism are inherently detrimental to national cohesion, as they hinder the process of nation-building. In India, religious nationalism – especially the Hindutva ideology cloaked in cultural terms – represents a divisive and sectarian agenda. In contrast, true Indian nationalism is inclusive, embracing all citizens, regardless of their cultural or religious backgrounds.

### Deinstitutionalising Meritocratic Plausibility Structures through Redignification of Ordinary Life
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20251592](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20251592)
- **Analytical Tags**: `OT / - / Contemporary`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  According to recent sociological analyses, the bottom fourth of the American income bracket is ‘dechurching’ faster and in larger numbers than any other demographic. Many factors play into this exodus: it is my belief that the West’s contemporary strain of meritocracy, which imbues those who succeed with a hubris and those who fail with humiliation, is the primary culprit for this alienation. The plausibility structures of upward social mobility cause winners to attribute their success to their hard work alone, while the losers attribute failure to their own lack of hard work. This prevailing framework implicitly embeds itself within ecclesial orthopraxy. In order to deinstitutionalise these deeply ingrained beliefs, meritocratic hubris should be countered by a redignification of all varieties of work, pedigree, and social position. This article argues that the ecclesial context is the ideal axiom for deconstructing plausibility structures that broadcast the universal accessibility of upward social mobility within Western society.

### John Milbank and the Fortunes of Postliberal Theology
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20251614](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20251614)
- **Analytical Tags**: `Systematic Theology / - / Modern`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  The article offers a critical evaluation of John Milbank’s advocacy of postliberal theology in his writings of the last three decades. His critique of secularism and liberalism is found to be deficient in several regards and counter arguments for a critical but positive engagement with secular modernity are advanced. Liberal democracy should not be equated with possessive individualism because it involves obligations, solidarities, and the sustaining of civic life by good citizenship and virtuous traditions. The virulent rejection of the secular by Milbank, Stanley Hauerwas and Rowan Williams, misunderstands the relationship between the sacred and secular. The sacred embraces every sphere of life including the secular realm and Milbank’s account of the secular is not just inaccurate but theologically unwarranted, especially in relation to the doctrine of creation and incarnational theology.

### Public Theology and Global Catastrophic Risk
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20251616](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20251616)
- **Analytical Tags**: `Systematic Theology / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
                  We live, by virtue of an unprecedented interconnectedness driven by massively uneven, wealth-dominated processes of globalisation, in an era of global risk for which human beings must take responsibility and to which they must respond collectively. This article asks whether public theology – which is essentially a process of theologically informed dialogue with different parts of society on matters of shared concern – has a role to play in crafting these responses. It takes the answer to this question to be yes because the alternative – an all-encompassing, thoroughly autonomous humanism – will never on its own be sufficient to deliver us from hubris, violence, and excess. Public theology brings a different tone to these conversations by emphasising the unity of creation and the commonsense virtues of sustainability, stewardship, and the common good without underestimating the strength and persistence of the human drive to survive. It speaks both pragmatically and prophetically to our potentially catastrophic future without sliding into millennialism and draws on its grasp of the more than human reality that encompasses us to reveal a way forward that provides some real measure of relief from humanity’s prevailing materialist paradigm.

### The Public Pulpit of Johan Cilliers

                  South African pastor, professor and homiletician Johan Cilliers advocates for the capacity of preaching to shape a healing and healthy political imagination among God’s people. ### Religion as ‘Social Good’: Historical Backgrounds for Public Theology
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20251631](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20251631)
- **Analytical Tags**: `Church History / - / Medieval;Modern`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
This article explores the evolving perception of religion as a social good within public theology against the backdrop of modern secularism. Beginning with the historical shift towards hostility against religion, particularly from the Enlightenment and subsequent movements like Marxism and new atheism, the article traces how religion’s public role diminished. However, it argues that religion is re-emerging as a positive societal force, even as it is once again severely criticized for the ideological uses to which it has been subjected. Drawing on medieval theological concepts, particularly Thomas Aquinas’ view of religio as a virtue, the article posits that religion is integral to societal well-being and personal flourishing. It critiques Enlightenment-driven reductions of religion to mere ideology and asserts that modern empirical studies demonstrate the benefits of religion, including fostering altruism and social cohesion. By contributing to saving a non-romanticized and non-reductive perception of religion, this article also highlights its positive social role, an important element in thinking about public theology. It concludes that restoring religion’s public role, especially through a framework of virtue, can counter the secular narrative of its harmfulness and contribute meaningfully to social life.

### Prayer Processions as Political Protests in Postsecular Serbia (2014–2024)
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20251633](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20251633)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
Ever since the fall of Milošević’s ideologically hybrid regime in 2000, and the final disintegration of Yugoslavia, postsocialist, post-Yugoslav, post-Non-Aligned Serbia has been in the process of negotiating and shaping its modern national identity. This has often included fierce symbolic struggles, especially in terms of ownership over public space and, most notably, surrounding Gay Pride Parades: an early response was a prayer procession organized to ‘purge’ the streets after the first successful manifestation of this sort. This article strives to offer a brief overview of the developments in the use of prayer processions over the past decade. These processions have been politically charged religious rituals performed in the public space with specific demands posed to the state, but rarely have they been directly organized by the official church. Special attention will be accorded to the (increasingly explicit) geopolitical implications of this phenomenon.

### Religion and Society: the Evolutionary and Biological Connection
- **DOI**: [10.1163/15697320-20251640](https://doi.org/10.1163/15697320-20251640)
- **Analytical Tags**: `NT / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract
The interplay between society, culture, and religion has significantly shaped humanity throughout history.
