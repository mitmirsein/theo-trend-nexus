### A Neo-Confucian Theology of Liberation Humanism and Ethics in Levinas Liberation Theology and Wang Yangming
- **DOI**: [10.1017/s0017816025000069](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0017816025000069)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / Method / -`
- **Abstract**: <jats:title>Abstract</jats:title><jats:p>This article reconsiders the sixteenth-century Idealist Neo-Confucian philosophy of Wang Yangming (1472–1529) in light of the development of twentieth-century Latin American liberation theology. After defining liberation theology, this study identifies the crucial contributions made to it by Emmanuel Levinas’s assertion of the primacy of ethics over ontology and critique of the egocentric nature of Western philosophy. It then delineates the epistemological and deontological criticisms made of Roman Catholic orthodoxy—and institutionalized Christianity in general—by Latin American liberation theologians, particularly Enrique Dussel and José Porfirio Miranda. Thus, contrary to the conventional view of Confucianism as a conservative philosophy, these elements in Wang’s Neo-Confucianism render it a theology (or philosophy) of liberation.</jats:p>

### Transforming Mortality Pauls Linguistic Expressions and Thought Development from 1 Cor 1213 to 2 Cor 4712

- **Abstract**: <jats:title>Abstract</jats:title><jats:p>This article re-thinks the development of Paul’s thought between 1 and 2 Corinthians. ### Abraham David and the Problem of Exile in Calvins Theology

- **Abstract**: <jats:title>Abstract</jats:title><jats:p>Scholars have long noted the prevalence of exile as a theme in John Calvin’s theology, which responded to times unsettled by religious persecution and migration.
