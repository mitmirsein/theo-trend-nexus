### Authentic Subjectivity as a Lens for Christians Studying Spirituality

- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: This essay makes a case for studying spirituality through the lens of the spirit of authenticity in human subjectivity. It does so in the context of the need today to give greater priority to reaching out to spiritual seekers who are not at home with Christian spirituality and who would be put off, therefore, by approaches of an explicitly Christian kind. Authentic subjectivity, however, also provides the basis for the Christian spirituality scholar-practitioner to draw on explicit Christian resources, when it serves their context, interlocutors, and more-than-human world.

### Decolonizing Contemplation: Toward a Nonviolent Gaze
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956946](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956946)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: Contemplation, as the act of gazing or beholding the other, is often entangled with the colonial white gaze that objectifies the other. This essay attempts to revisit the notion of Christian contemplation by arguing that decolonizing contemplation requires a shift toward a nonviolent gaze. By utilizing Frantz Fanon’s analysis of the black colonized subjects, I reframe contemplation as a transcending act of delinking from the Western colonial matrix and relinking to the voices and epistemologies from the margin. Thus, contemplation must involve two gazes: attentiveness to the woundedness of the self and the world, and embracing the other.

### Awakening to the Souls Among Us: Three Models of Integration with the More-Than-Human World
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956947](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956947)
- **Analytical Tags**: `Systematic Theology / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: Grounded in an understanding of Christ’s personhood as defined by relationship, this essay supplies three models to attune to the living quality of the more-than-human world. By drawing on St. Francis’s stance of kinship toward the created world, James Hillman’s interpretation of Plato’s anima mundi (world ensouled), and Classical Chinese Medicine’s Five Phase Element Theory, the essay develops language and imagination to overcome the habits of mind driving the “technocratic paradigm,” and to cultivate an awareness of the interconnectedness of creation.

### Spirituality to Change the World: A Response to the Prize Essays
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956948](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956948)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: Like much recent scholarship in Christian spirituality, the 2024 Founders’ Circle Prize essays by Febrianto and Allison Tao take an approach that can be described as activist, engaged, practical, or prophetic. This response situates the methodology of those essays in relation to the threefold typology of approaches (theological, historical-contextual, and hermeneutical) identified in the 1990s by Bernard McGinn and Sandra Schneiders. It may be time to recognize a significant methodological shift in which the horizons of the discipline are being expanded beyond the hermeneutical goals of understanding and personal transformation to stretch out toward advocacy and public action.

### Trees and the Life of Prayer: An Interspecies Sharing of Praise and Lament
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956949](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956949)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: Trees play a significant role in the history of prayer. The folklore of faith traditions everywhere make it clear that prayer is more than a human impulse. This essay examines the history of trees and prayer in Christian spirituality—from the early Dendrites (monks living in trees) to Thomas Merton’s life among the pines near his hermitage. It argues for an expansion of consciousness in “communicating” with trees, shows how they share in the biblical practice of praise and lament, recounts the cultural and mythic appropriation of specific trees, and surveys the new tree science with its emphasis on community.

### Spirituality Studies as a Discipline: The Contribution of Kees Waaijman
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956950](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956950)
- **Analytical Tags**: `Comparative Religion / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: This essay highlights the important contribution of Dutch scholar Kees Waaijman (1942–2023) to the academic recognition of spirituality studies, which has grown from the margins of religious studies into a recognized field. Given his pivotal role, Waaijman’s relatively unknown design deserves more attention. He advocated for integrating theory with spiritual practice through a phenomenological-dialogical method addressing foundational, methodological, and practical perspectives. Waaijman emphasized that academic research enriches and is enriched by lived spirituality. His work demonstrates how rigorous scholarship can deepen spiritual transformation, bridging theory and practice to illuminate and guide spirituality as lived experience.

### In Praise of God: The Life and Work of Professor Kees Waaijman, O.Carm
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956951](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956951)
- **Analytical Tags**: `OT / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: This essay reviews the life and work of Kees Waaijman, a pioneering and influential Dutch Carmelite scholar of Christian spirituality. It highlights the distinctively interdisciplinary character of his intellectual endeavors, and how his versatile scholarly initiatives were energized by a life-long devotion to the biblical Psalms and ultimately to the praise of God. Throughout his long and prolific career at the Titus Brandsma Institute in Nijmegen, the Netherlands, Waaijman perceived the God–human relationship as the experiential heart of Christian spirituality, all the while seeking ever more satisfactory integration of rigorous theory and socially engaged praxis.

### The Mystic Mode: Learning Alongside Professor Capps
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956952](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956952)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: This essay is an encomium to Walter Capps as a formative academic mentor and lifelong friend. Capps’s orientation to religious scholarship, and indeed to all of life, is depicted as inhabiting a “mystic mode”—that is, a mode of humble receptivity to mystery, eager engagement with extravagantly diverse dimensions of reality, and attunement to both the interior life and history’s ongoing movements. As a student, the author found Capps’s “mystic” life orientation illuminating and compelling. Thus the values and tonality that the essay highlights also came to guide the author’s own life and career. Such testimony implicitly underscores the transformational potential of good mentors.

### The Spiritual Life of AI (Imagined by Way of R. S. Thomas)
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956953](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956953)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: R. S. Thomas’s poem “The Empty Church” speaks to spiritual life in the age of artificial intelligence—even, in some ways, the spiritual life of artificial intelligence. It helps frame scholarship by media historians who describe the emergence of AI as a post–World War Two effort to mitigate the collective effects of trauma. As such thinking went, traumatized human neural networks might bolster faulty, even broken human decision-making. But “The Empty Church” reveals how such an approach actually intensifies the trauma it seeks to alleviate, mapping onto the distressing disappearance of God a disconcerting disappearance of humanity itself.

### Sewing and Gleaning: Artistic Contemplative Practice as Invitation
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a956954](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a956954)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / -`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: The biblical concept of leaving the edges of the harvest for others in need frames an exploration of contemplative artmaking and community engagement. Artmakers are contemplative “sowers” who create gleaning spaces. Publicly offered, they invite strangers to moments of contemplation. An exemplar project, entitled Pocket Wisdom, demonstrates the concept in practice. This essay highlights stitching as a pathway for contemplative practice, and artmaking as an opportunity for communities and individuals to find moments of contemplation. The artmaker “sews and sows,” and the person who beholds it “gleans.”

### Religion vs. Spirituality: A Contemporary Conundrum
- **DOI**: [10.1353/scs.2025.a972504](https://doi.org/10.1353/scs.2025.a972504)
- **Analytical Tags**: `- / - / Contemporary`
- **Abstract**: Abstract: Religion today appears in trouble while spirituality is in ascendency. This essay proposes a partnership model for their frequently vexed relationship. It begins with nuanced profiles of religion and spirituality, and points out that religious institutions emerge to perpetuate religious traditions and the spiritualities that enliven them. Unfortunately, such institutions can end up obliterating the values and experience they were established to nurture. Historic tensions between religion and spirituality are now exacerbated by postmodernity and various ideological criticisms of religion. Nonetheless, the essay concludes that (a chastened) religion remains the optimal context for Christian spirituality to flourish.
